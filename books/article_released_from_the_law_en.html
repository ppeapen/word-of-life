﻿The Metaphor Of Marriage In Romans, Chapter7

Alex M. Kuruvilla

Note: Please read Romans chapter 7 before and after reading this material.

First, the essential background:

✒️ ‘The Law’ referred to in Romans is not a reference to the general principle of all laws, but rather to the Torah (the first five books, also known as the Pentateuch). In particular, the term ‘the law’ refers to the Ten Commandments, which are at the core of the Torah.

✒️ Chapter seven opens with a question: ‘Or do you not know, brethren (for I speak to those who know the law), that the law has dominion over a man as long as he lives?’ What does it mean? It means that the Ten Commandments exercise lordship over a man as long as he lives. The Ten Commandments, which are the core of the law (the Torah), are binding on all people irrespective of caste, creed, and colour (Romans 2:13-15). Everyone, whether Jew or non-Jew, comes under the dominion of the Ten Commandments, since they form the moral laws of God.

✒️ Jesus Christ, the Son of God, was sent, subjecting Himself to the authority of the Law (TCs). We read: ‘But when the fullness of the time had come, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under the law,’ (Galatians 4:4). ‘Born under the Law’ means born under the dominion of the Law.

✒️ The validity of the law (the TCs) is perpetual; it never ends. As God is unchanging, so are His oracles and decrees. The Ten Commandments cannot be annulled, modified, or revoked.

✒️ Violation of any one of the Ten Commandments by desire and/or deed results in Sin, and Sin, in turn, causes ‘death’. Thus, sin reigned in death.

✒️ The Jewish law of divorce is found in Deuteronomy 24:1. According to that law, a married woman is bound to her husband as long as he lives. Under that law, only the husband had the right of divorce. The married woman had no right of divorce.

✒️ If the married woman becomes another man’s whilst her husband lives, she will be named ‘adulteress’.

✒️ But if the husband dies, she is free from the law of marriage. Note that the freedom is from the law of marriage and not from the husband.

✒️ Our Savior Jesus Christ, the Lamb of God, was sinless. He did no sin; He knew no sin; And in Him there was no sin. In His capacity as a man, He comes under the dominion of the law. When He took upon Himself the guilt of the world’s sin, He had to undergo God’s punishment, which is death. He died for us and on our behalf, bearing the guilt of our sin. Since He is sinless, the just God raised Him from the dead on the third day, and now He lives forever. Thus, Jesus liberated Himself from the law. Now the Law has no dominion over Him. And consequently, death has no power over Him.

The metaphor:

✒️ For the sake of illustration, let us say that I am (the writer) a married woman. My husband is Mr. Law. I am bound by the law of marriage to be his as long as he lives.

✒️ Mr. Law is a perfector. He demands perfection all the time. And he is closely watching me. He finds me guilty of wrongdoing in everything that I do. If I make the bed, he will find faults. If I cook, he will find faults. If I clean the home, he will find faults. Sitting on the couch, his job is 24x7: finding my faults. And he is not willing to lift a finger to move my buren.

✒️ As long as he lives, I am bound to him. I am fed up with this married life. I really want to divorce him. But I have no right of divorce under the law of marriage. So, I earnestly desired his death. But he does not die. He is perpetual. I am frustrated since there is no way to secure freedom from my husband, Mr. Law.

✒️ If my husband does not die, the only option left is that I die. If I die, I will be free from the law of marriage. But that will not fulfill the purpose of my life. After freeing myself from Mr. Law through death, I want to live again to enjoy the fullness of life with another husband. But how is that possible?

✒️ Then I heard of a Sviour. His name is Jesus Christ. He was also under the dominion of the Law, the Ten Commandments. He obeyed God in all respects and in all aspects. Thus, He secured righteousness by keeping the commandments. Bearing the guilt of my sin, sinless Jesus died for and on my behalf.

✒️ By His death, Jesus liberated Himself from the dominion of the Law. He was buried and resurrected on the third day. He now lives forevermore.

✒️ If I can partake in His death, burial, and resurrection, I can also be free from Mr. Law (i.e., freedom from the dominion of the law - the Ten Commandments). Not only that, freed from the law of marriage, I can become His wife. Thus, I can live forevermore without being labeled as an adulteress.

✒️ Now comes the crucial question: “How can I partake in Jesus’ death?” A divine intervention is necessary, without which I cannot participate in the death of Christ. Verse 4 of chapter 7 says, ‘Therefore, my brethren, you also were made to die to the Law through the body of Christ, so that you might be joined to another, to Him who was raised from the dead, so that we might bear fruit for God’ (NASB 1995). “So that, my brethren, ye also were made dead to the law through the body of the Christ, for your becoming another’s, who out of the dead was raised up, that we might bear fruit to God” (YLT98). Note the passive voice of the verb ‘to make’ there. Who made me die to the Law, and who joined me to Christ? It is God the Father (Ephesians 2: 8; Romans 2:4).

✒️ By partaking in Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection, I can be united to Him. I can be united to Jesus through repentance, belief (i.e., belief in the person of Jesus – His name – His title, John 3:16-18), in His works of salvation, and baptism. Baptism is a symbolic enactment ordained by God that helps me unite with Jesus’ death, burial, and resurrection (Romans 6:3-11).

✒️ How does baptism help me to participate in the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus? What is the logic here? To understand this, we must first examine the meaning of 1 Peter 3:18-21. Verse 21 states:

  • ‘This water (i.e., the flood water) symbolizes baptism’ (NIV);
  • ‘Baptism, which corresponds to this (i.e., the flood water) (ESV);
  • ‘There is also an antitype (പ്രതിരൂപം) which now saves us—baptism’ (NKJV).

✒️ God judged the people of Noah’s time for their sins by sending a global flood. The floodwater was God’s weapon of judgment. From the above-quoted scripture, it becomes clearer that God appointed baptismal water as a sign of His judgment. The baptism of Jesus by John the Baptist was a symbolic proclamation that the Son of God was willing to submit Himself to God’s judgment for the people’s sins. Thus, Jesus surrendered to fulfill God’s righteousness (Matthew 3:15).

✒️ So, when you undergo water baptism with true repentance and faith, and according to the prescription of the gospels, i.e., when you immerse yourself in the baptismal water, the sign of God’s judgment, you are united to Christ in His death. He suffered death for your sake. Thus, baptism, administered in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit (Matthew 28:19), is a symbolic enactment with a guaranteed spiritual benefit. The spiritual efficacy of baptism is that you are united to Christ not only in His death, but also in His burial and resurrection.

✒️ Finally, I got baptized, i.e., I participated in the death of Christ. Thus, I am released from the law of marriage (my cruel husband). And now I am united to my new husband, Jesus Christ. We started our new married life. But there is a huge problem. I found that my new husband is also a perfectionist, to a greater extent than my previous husband, Mr. Law. Unlike Mr. Law, Jesus is not imposing the Ten Commandments on me. Instead, He demands that I obey only two commands. They are: 1) You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind and with all your strength, and 2) You shall love your neighbor as yourself (Mark 12:30-31). My ‘neighbor’ includes my enemies also. Loving my enemy is contrary to my nature. How can I love my enemy? Furthermore, Jesus requires me to produce fruit for God the Father.

✒️ But Jesus is quite different from my first husband. Mr. Law did not help me to obey the commandments. Whereas Jesus helps me continually keep those two commands and bear fruit for the glory of God the Father.

✒️ How did Jesus help me? Jesus sent His Spirit (Holy Spirit) as another helper to abide in me and help me obey God. ‘For the law of the Spirit of life has set you free in Christ Jesus from the law of sin and death ' (Romans 8:2). 'But if you are led by the Spirit, you are not under the law’ (Galatians 5:18).
