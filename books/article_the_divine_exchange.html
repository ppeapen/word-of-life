The Divine Exchange At The Cross

(Exposition of Romans 8:1-4)

Alex M. Kuruvilla

Scripture:

'There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit. For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has made me free from the law of sin and death. For what the law could not do in that it was weak through the flesh, God did by sending His own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, on account of sin: He condemned sin in the flesh, that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit.' (Romans 8:1-4 NKJV).

'There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus.”

  • There is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, i.e., there is no judgment against those sinners who are in Christ Jesus.
  • ‘In Christ Jesus’ means those who are united to Christ by participating in His death, burial, and resurrection through belief, which is outwardly expressed through baptism (Romans 6:3-11).
  • Why no condemnation? Because the sinner’s guilt has already been punished in the body of Jesus Christ (the likeness of sinful flesh). Since the sinner is united with Christ through baptism, he participates in the punishment that Jesus suffered. He participates in the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus Christ. And God never punishes the same guilt twice. The participation and union with Jesus Christ is the spiritual efficacy of baptism. It happens in the spiritual realm.

‘Who do not walk according to the flesh, but according to the Spirit’.

  • The term ‘the Spirit’ refers to the Holy Spirit, the gift of God. Sinners who are united to Christ through baptism have the gift of the Holy Spirit (Acts 2:38). The Holy Spirit abides in them as another helper. Therefore, they walk according to the Holy Spirit, meaning the Spirit helps them to conduct themselves in the ways of God.

‘For the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has made me free from the law of sin and death.’

  • What does ‘the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus' mean? God gives the gift of the Holy Spirit to the repentant, baptized believer at the very moment he is raised from the water of baptism. We read, “Then Peter said to them, 'Repent, and let every one of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins; and you shall receive the gift of the Holy Spirit.' (Acts 2:38 NKJV). The promise of the gift of the Holy Spirit is fulfilled then and there.
  • What does ‘the law of sin and death’ mean? Sin is moral lawlessness (or disobedience of the Ten Commandments, the moral laws of God). We read, 'Whoever commits sin also commits lawlessness, and sin is lawlessness. (I John 3:4 NKJV). 'Everyone who sins breaks the law; in fact, sin is lawlessness. (1 John 3:4 NIV). The consequence of sin is ‘death’, i.e., separation from God. Sin and death are an inseparable couple, functioning together. Hence, the term ‘the law of sin and death’.
  • What does ‘made me free from the law of sin and death' mean? The law of the Spirit of life in Christ freed me from the lordship (domain) of the Ten Commandments. In chapter 7, we see the metaphor of marriage. The sinner is released from the Law by uniting himself with Jesus Christ through baptism (Romans 6:3-11; 7:4).
  • Released from one law (i.e., the law of sin and death), the forgiven sinner comes under the domain of another law, i.e., the law of the Spirit of life in Christ.
  • Illustration: A piece of metal, by itself, cannot be suspended in the air. Since that piece of metal is subject to gravity, it will surely fall to the ground. But if the same piece of metal is subjected to aerodynamic forces, it can overcome gravity and remain airborne. Likewise, a saved sinner in Christ Jesus overcomes the drag of the law of sin and death (the sinful nature) by subjecting himself to the law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus.

‘For what the law could not do in that it was weak through the flesh, …’

  • Theoretically, one can be righteous by obeying the Ten Commandments both in the intent of the heart and by deeds. The sacred scripture confirms this truth. We read, “For it is not those who hear the law who are righteous in God’s sight, but it is those who obey the law (the Ten Commandments) who will be declared righteous.” (Romans 2:13 NIV).
  • But, sadly, no one can obey the Ten Commandments in their entirety due to the inherited sinful nature, described as ‘flesh’. The tenth commandment is ‘You shall not covet.’ Because of our sinful nature (flesh), we continually violate the first nine commandments of the Ten Commandments by covetousness (inordinate desire/greed), the tenth commandment. That then is the weakness of the law (or TCs). Paul confirms this understanding. He said, “Well then, am I suggesting that the law of God is sinful? Of course not! In fact, it was the law that showed me my sin. I would never have known that coveting is wrong if the law had not said, “You must not covet.” (Romans 7:7 NLT). “You shall not covet” is the tenth commandment.
  • It shows that the Adamic race, due to the inward distortions (congenital heart condition (Jeremiah 17: 9), is totally incapable of obeying God. No matter how good your outward actions are, inwardly you are corrupt — sinful. This condition is called the ‘ Total Depravity’ (also called radical corruption) of man. Therefore, an outside supernatural intervention is necessary for a sinner to repent and believe (Ephesians 2:8-9; Romans 2: 4). This supernatural intervention is called ‘regeneration’.

No matter how good your outward actions are, inwardly you are corrupt — sinful. This condition is called the ‘ Total Depravity’ (also called radical corruption) of man. Therefore, an outside supernatural intervention is necessary for a sinner to repent and believe (Ephesians 2:8-9; Romans 2: 4). This supernatural intervention is called ‘regeneration’.
  • “God did” shows that an outward intervention was necessary to change the deceitful human heart. He did this by sending His Son in “the likeness of sinful flesh” (Romans 8:3)—that is, by taking upon Himself true human nature, yet without sin. Here, we catch a glimpse of the mystery of the Person of Jesus Christ. Jesus of Nazareth is none other than God the Son, who, without ceasing to be God, took to Himself a true human nature in the womb of the virgin Mary by the power of the Holy Spirit. Thus, the eternal Son of God became truly human and appeared among us in human form (Philippians 2:6–8).
  • The purpose of sending God’s Son in the likeness of sinful flesh is to deal with man’s sin once and for all (Matthew 1:21; Hebrews 9:11; 10:11).

‘He condemned sin in the flesh.’

  • This means that God judged sin in the flesh of Jesus Christ, who came in the likeness of sinful human flesh. Jesus Himself was completely without sin. Yet He willingly stood before God, the Supreme Judge, as the Substitute for sinners, bearing the guilt of sin and submitting Himself to the punishment that sin deserved. In doing so, He became the perfect sacrifice and Substitute for humanity, bearing our sins in His body on the cross, while remaining perfectly sinless Himself.
  • This is God’s general offer of salvation to humanity through Jesus Christ. Yet the saving benefits of Christ’s death are not automatically received by everyone. They must be personally appropriated through repentance and faith in Christ. The believer is united with Christ in His death, burial, and resurrection, which is outwardly expressed through baptism.

‘That the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit.'

  • God requires that sin be punished with death. That is the righteous requirement of a just God, which in turn is the requirement of the law (TCs). The sacred scripture attests to this truth. We read:
    • 'Behold, all souls are mine; the soul of the father as well as the soul of the son is mine: the soul who sins shall die. ' (Ezekiel 18:4).
    • 'For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord.' (Romans 6:23)
  • The requirement of the law (i.e., that the sinner must die) is accomplished by God Himself for the repentant, baptized believer. How? The repentant believer was made to die to the Law through the body of Christ (Romans 7: 4 - NASB; DARBY; LSV; YLT98; WMBBE) in baptism. It is the Holy Spirit (the gift of God) who fulfills this requirement in the spiritual realm through the believer's faith. This, then, is the spiritual efficacy of ‘Believer’s Baptism’.

Conclusion

What happened at the Cross was a divine exchange. Christ, the obedient Son of God, took upon Himself the curse that we deserved for our disobedience, so that we might receive the blessings that belong to Him. He took our place and bore the judgment that our sins deserved, while we, through union with Him, receive the blessings of salvation that we could never earn by our own obedience.

We read:

'Christ has redeemed us from the curse of the law, having become a curse for us (for it is written, “Cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree”), that the blessing of Abraham might come upon the Gentiles in Christ Jesus, that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith.' (Galatians 3:13-14 NKJV).
