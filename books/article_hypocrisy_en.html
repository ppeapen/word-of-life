What Is Hypocrisy (കപടഭക്തി)?

What does the word hypocrisy mean in the language of the Bible? This is not a question believers can afford to ignore. Every child of God must understand it clearly—because hypocrisy is something God strongly detests.

During His earthly ministry, our Lord Jesus Christ repeatedly confronted the Pharisees. These religious leaders appeared outwardly devout, yet Jesus did not hesitate to expose their true condition. On several occasions, He openly called them “hypocrites” (Matthew 23:13–36). His words were not casual criticism; they were a solemn warning about a dangerous spiritual condition.

Rather than offering a human definition, it is far better to learn what hypocrisy means from Jesus Himself. Speaking to the Pharisees, He said:

“Hypocrites! Well did Isaiah prophesy about you, saying: ‘These people draw near to Me with their mouth and honor Me with their lips, but their heart is far from Me’” (Matthew 15:7–8).

Similarly, in another place, Jesus declared:

“Well did Isaiah prophesy of you hypocrites, as it is written: ‘This people honors Me with their lips, but their heart is far from Me’” (Mark 7:6).

From these words, the meaning becomes unmistakably clear. In biblical terms, hypocrisy is offering God words without giving Him the heart. It is drawing near to Him with our lips while our hearts remain distant. This can easily happen during prayer or worship. We may speak many words, yet our hearts may not be engaged with what we are saying. Our mouths continue speaking, but our minds wander, and our affections drift. When this happens, we are merely offering lip service to God.

This is a serious matter in the sight of God—and it is also a difficult struggle for us as believers. Even when we sincerely attempt to pray with focused hearts, we often discover how quickly our thoughts wander. Halfway through our prayer, the heart begins to drift. Therefore, instead of pretending otherwise, we must humbly acknowledge this weakness before God and confess it to Him.

God listens far more attentively to the condition of our hearts than to the sound of our lips.
