﻿﻿﻿The Death of Death

In the Death of Christ

Alex M. Kuruvilla

1. What is death?

Who introduced the concept of “death” to humanity, and what does it mean? It was God, the Creator, who first spoke of death. He created man in His own image and likeness, enabling man to reflect Him within the created order. God commanded the first parents: “Of every tree of the garden you may freely eat; but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die.” (Genesis 2:16-17). Thus, God declared death the inevitable consequence of disobedience.

But what is death? To understand this, we must consider humanity's condition before the fall. Adam and Eve, though embodied beings, lived in uninterrupted fellowship with their Creator. God was continually present to them; there was no separation between them and Him. Human existence, by its very nature, is dependent upon God. Apart from Him, man cannot truly live or flourish. This relationship may be compared to that of an unborn child sustained by its mother through the umbilical cord. In this sense, separation from the Creator is contrary to man’s design.

Death, therefore, is not annihilation but separation from God. It is the loss of communion with Him while existence continues. For the human soul, such separation is a state of profound and unending distress, like being in an everlasting fire yet not being consumed by it.

2. How did death enter humanity?

Disobedience, at its core, is rebellion against God—a rejection of His authority and a desire to displace Him. The Bible defines such rebellion as sin. Sin is not merely a moral failure; it is an offense against God Himself. Its inevitable consequence is death, understood not only as physical mortality but, more importantly, as separation from God.

Scripture explains the origin of this condition: “Therefore, just as through one man sin entered the world, and death through sin, and thus death spread to all men, because all sinned” (Romans 5:12). Through Adam’s transgression, sin entered the human race, and death followed as its consequence. Because all have sinned, all stand under the sentence of death.

This separation is further described in Isaiah: “Your sins have separated you from your God; your sins have hidden His face from you” (Isaiah 59:2). Thus, death is not merely the end of physical life but the rupture of fellowship between humanity and God.

3. How did it happen?

God alone is truly good. He is both the source and the perfect expression of all goodness. As Jesus declared, “No one is good—except God alone” (Mark 10:18). ‘Good’ is a title that belongs to God only.

Yet Scripture also reveals the existence of a created spiritual being who, by his own will, rebelled against God. Desiring God’s position, authority, and worship, he sought to exalt himself. Through this rebellion, he became Satan—the adversary—also called the Devil, the one who opposes God. If God is the source of all that is good, Satan stands as the origin and embodiment of evil.

God created humanity with a purpose: to bear His image and to exercise dominion (His kingdom) on earth as His representatives. Placed in the Garden of Eden, man was entrusted with this role under God’s authority. However, God’s command also set a boundary: “You may freely eat of every tree of the garden; but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil you shall not eat, for in the day that you eat of it you shall surely die.” This command was not restrictive in a negative sense but protective. Had humanity obeyed, they would have remained in unbroken fellowship with God, continually sharing in His life.

Satan seized upon this command as an opportunity to subvert God’s design. Through deception, he enticed humanity to disobey by appealing to the desire for autonomy—“you will be like God.” In doing so, he led man into rebellion, and through that rebellion, into death. As a consequence, the LORD God expelled man from the Garden of Eden and barred him from the way to life—that is, the life of God (Genesis 3:22–24). A great gulf now exists between God and man, and man’s knowledge of God has been reduced to a dim and fading awareness.

We should know that Satan is continually engaging the same strategy to separate people from God. He causes man to sin by enticing him to violate God’s Ten Commandments, the moral laws of God, through his lust (or coveteousness), words, or deeds.

Jesus described Satan’s nature in stark terms: “He was a murderer from the beginning… for he is a liar and the father of lies” (John 8:44). By his deception, sin entered the human story, and death followed, bringing separation from God to all humanity.

4. Did God fail?

Now a question arises: Did God fail? Certainly not. Is He weaker than Satan? No, He is not. Then why did He allow Satan to enter the Garden? Because God had a better strategy than Satan’s. His strategy is to destroy the works of Satan once and for all. His objective is to remove the possibility of death (that is, separation from Him) for all who believe and respond positively to His rescue plan.

It was disobedience that brought about death (separation); it is obedience that cancels death. Indeed, God is directing human history toward the fulfillment of this end. Yet it is a sobering reality that not all will be saved. There is no universal redemption. The blood of Jesus, shed as propitiation and expiation for the guilt of human sin, benefits only the repentant believer—not the unrepentant who rejects the Rescuer.

God’s strategy also includes the just recompense of His adversary, Satan, who will be consigned to eternal torment in hellfire.

5. The Death of Death in the Death of Christ

Jesus conquered death:- Jesus, being in the likeness of sinful flesh (nature) or in the likeness of men, conquered death. How? Jesus was liable to sin in His body of sinful nature. Satan tempted Him continually to disobey God. But Jesus did not fall to those temptations. He obeyed God to the point of death, even the death of the cross (Philippians 2:8). Thus, in His capacity as a man, He earned the righteousness of God that qualified Him to be raised from the dead on the third day. Now Jesus lives evermore; death has no power over Him. There is no longer a separation between the last Adam (or the second Man) and God. Jesus Himself testifies to this truth: … “I am the First and the Last. I am He who lives, and was dead, and behold, I am alive forevermore. Amen. And I have the keys of Hades and of Death. ' (Revelation 1:17-18).

More than that, in His role as the Second Man—the Last Adam—Jesus received all authority in heaven and on earth and was bestowed the supreme title, “Lord.” The authority, power, and honor granted to Him on His resurrection far surpass those given to the first Adam. Consequently, Jesus became the head of a new humanity, whose source and Father is God. God is now at work transferring people from the old Adamic order into this new humanity in Christ through a supernatural birth—being “born again”—brought about through the gospel of Jesus Christ.

Jesus abolished death by His death: - Jesus abolished death by reversing what Satan had accomplished through deception and disobedience. Satan used God’s command as an occasion to lead humanity into rebellion, and through that rebellion came death—that is, separation from God. In contrast, Jesus Christ, the Son of Man, perfectly obeyed the will of God in both letter and spirit, thereby opening the way for fallen humanity to be reconciled and united with the holy God.

Prophet Isaiah prophesied that the coming Messiah would swallow up death forever. We read: 'He (i.e., the coming Savior) will swallow up death forever, And the Lord God will wipe away tears from all faces; ……. For the Lord has spoken' (Isaiah 25:8 NKJV).

Because of Jesus’ obedience to God the Father, death had no rightful claim over Him. Therefore, God raised Him from the dead. Through His death and resurrection, Christ conquered death and became a life-giving Spirit to those who were spiritually dead and separated from God.

How did Jesus abolish death for the believer? When a believer enters into union with Christ—that is, into His death, burial, and resurrection—he partakes of the everlasting life of Christ Himself. Just as Christ is united with the Father, so also are true believers united with Him.

'The Scriptures tell us, 'The first man, Adam, became a living person.” But the last Adam—that is, Christ—is a life-giving Spirit ' (1 Corinthians 15:45 NLT).

The Scriptures also attest that Jesus abolished death through His obedience. We read:

“…but has now been revealed by the appearing of our Savior Jesus Christ, who has abolished death and brought life and immortality to light through the gospel…” (II Timothy 1:10 NKJV)

“Therefore, as one trespass led to condemnation for all men, so one act of righteousness leads to justification and life for all men. For as by the one man’s disobedience the many were made sinners, so by the one man’s obedience the many will be made righteous.” (Romans 5:18–19 ESV)

'Since the children have flesh and blood, he too shared in their humanity so that by his death (i.e., the death of Jesus) he might break the power of him who holds the power of death—that is, the devil— and free those who all their lives were held in slavery by their fear of death ' (Hebrews 2:14-15 NIV).

However, the benefits of Christ’s death and resurrection are not automatically applied to every person. A personal response of faith is necessary to receive salvation from God through Jesus Christ. These benefits are received through repentance and faith in the Lord Jesus Christ and in His atoning work.

That personal response is expressed through obedience to Jesus’ command: “He who believes and is baptized will be saved; but he who does not believe will be condemned” (Mark 16:16). This response of faith finds expression in baptism by immersion, through which the believer is united with Christ in His death, burial, and resurrection (Romans 6:3–10). Only then does the sinner pass from death to life and enter into inseparable fellowship with God.

Therefore, let us always rejoice. Nothing can separate us from the love of God manifested through the death, burial, and resurrection of His Son, Jesus Christ.
