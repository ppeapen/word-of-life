Knowing God And Being Known By Him

Knowing God And Being Known By Him

Alex M. Kuruvilla

Knowledge Of God

God, as revealed in the Bible, is personal and therefore relational. He desires that His people know Him intimately and personally. Rather than remaining distant from humanity, He enters into a covenant relationship with His people. What is the nature of this covenant?

In the Bible, a covenant is a solemn, binding relationship God establishes with people, involving promises, commitments, and often signs or obligations. The Hebrew word berit and Greek diathēkē are commonly translated as “covenant.” A covenant is more than an ordinary contract. A contract is primarily an agreement about an exchange; a biblical covenant often establishes or defines a relationship—especially the relationship between God and His people.

Scripture portrays this covenant relationship as being like that of a husband and wife. Just as a husband comes to know his wife through a deep, personal, and intimate relationship, so God knows His people. In return, He calls His people to know Him with that same depth of intimacy and devotion. God demands that His covenant partner be faithful to Him.

God’s commitment under the covenant is called ‘loyal love’, ‘steadfast love’, ‘mercy’, or ‘lovingkindness '. The Hebrew word is ‘hesed’. In Lamentations 3:22-23, God’s committed love is portrayed in these words: 'The steadfast love of the Lord never ceases; his mercies never come to an end; they are new every morning; great is your faithfulness.' Those in this covenant relationship receive His steadfast love (His hesed), through which He faithfully pours out His blessings on His covenant partners.

For this reason, God invites every believer to pursue a personal, intimate relationship with Him, grow in knowing who He is, and experience the fullness of His covenant love. The crux of the Christian faith is knowing God and being known by Him. The following Bible verses attest to this truth:

Through the prophet Hosea, God Yahweh is urging His people to know Him. ‘Let us know; let us press on to know the Lord; his going out is sure as the dawn; he will come to us as the showers, as the spring rains that water the earth.’ (Hosea 6:3)

'For I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice, the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings.’ (Hosea 6:6). This verse shows that God values knowing Him more than burnt offerings.

Our calling in Jesus Christ is to become members of the royal priesthood (1 Peter 2:9). The inevitable consequence of lacking knowledge of God is being rejected as a priest. This bible verse attests to that truth: 'My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge (i.e., knowledge of God); because you have rejected knowledge (i.e., knowledge of God), I reject you from being a priest to me. And since you have forgotten the law of your God, I also will forget your children. (Hosea 4:6).

'The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, and the knowledge of the Holy One is understanding (or wisdom).’ (Proverbs 9:10)

'Thus says the Lord: “Let not the wise man boast in his wisdom, let not the mighty man boast in his might, let not the rich man boast in his riches, but let him who boasts boast in this, that he understands and knows me, that I am the Lord who practices steadfast love, justice, and righteousness in the earth. For in these things I delight, declares the Lord.” (Jeremiah 9:23-24)

'Oh, taste and see that the Lord is good! Blessed is the man who takes refuge in him! (Psalm 34:8).

'For thus says the Lord to the house of Israel: “Seek me and live;” (Amos 5:4).

Seeking involves knowing God intimately and personally.

On the night when our Lord was betrayed, He prayed,

'And this is eternal life, that they know You, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom You have sent' (John 17:3).

The Surpassing Worth of Knowing Christ Jesus

The apostle Paul (Jewish name: Saul) was a Pharisee and a Pharisee's son. He was zealous about his ancestral rich traditions. Historians testify that he was also a rich man. But he counted all these losses for the sake of knowing Jesus. Such is the surpassing worthiness of knowing Jesus Christ, our Lord.

Apostle Paul, writing to the Philippian church, said, “But whatever gain I had, I counted as loss for the sake of Christ. Indeed, I count everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus, my Lord. For his sake I have suffered the loss of all things and count them as rubbish, in order that I may gain Christ and be found in him, not having a righteousness of my own that comes from the law, but that which comes through faith in Christ, the righteousness from God that depends on faith— that I may know him and the power of his resurrection, and may share his sufferings, becoming like him in his death, that by any means possible I may attain the resurrection from the dead.” (Philippians 3:7-11).

Knowing Christ, the Basis of Christian Faith

The Christian faith is not founded on blind belief but on the sure knowledge of the historical Jesus, whom God sent in fulfillment of the Scriptures, and on the confession of Him as Lord (Matthew 16:15–16; Romans 10:9–10). Jesus Himself declared that eternal life consists in knowing the only true God and Jesus Christ, whom He sent to save sinners from their sins (John 17:3). Jesus Christ, the Son of God, reflects God the Father (2 Corinthians 4:6). Hence, knowing Jesus is knowing the very God Himself. Therefore, knowing Jesus Christ is essential to trusting Him and believing in His saving work.

This emphasis on knowing God is consistent throughout Scripture. As the Lord declares through the prophet Hosea, "For I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice, the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings" (Hosea 6:6). Although the LORD God Yahweh Himself ordained the burnt offering and described it as "a pleasing aroma" to Him (Leviticus 1: 9, 17), He values the knowledge of God even more. This shows that while outward acts of worship have their proper place, they never replace genuine, personal knowledge of God.

Consequence of Not Knowing God

Many people assume that knowing God simply means acquiring information about Him. However, in the Bible, knowing God means entering into a personal, intimate relationship with Him.

For example, Genesis 4:1 says, “Now Adam knew Eve his wife, and she conceived and bore Cain.” Here, the expression “knew Eve” does not mean that Adam merely possessed information about his wife. Rather, it signifies an intimate personal relationship. Likewise, God calls His people to know Him personally through a covenant relationship.

What if a person does not know God? What if he or she has no relationship with Him?

Scripture makes it clear that God does not remain indifferent toward those who refuse Him. Rather, He will one day judge those who neither know Him nor obey the gospel of Jesus Christ. Why is this so? Because God graciously invites people into a covenant relationship with Himself through His Son. Rejecting that invitation means rejecting God's gracious offer of reconciliation. Scripture describes believers as the bride of Christ, and becoming part of His bride means entering into a personal relationship with God. This is why Jesus declared:

“And this is eternal life, that they may know You, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom You have sent” (John 17:3).

Where does Scripture teach that God will judge those who do not know Him? The apostle Paul writes:

"This will happen when the Lord Jesus is revealed from heaven in blazing fire with his powerful angels. He will punish those who do not know God and do not obey the gospel of our Lord Jesus. They will be punished with everlasting destruction and shut out from the presence of the Lord and from the glory of his might” (2 Thessalonians 1:7b–9).

This passage plainly declares that those who do not know God and refuse to obey the gospel of the Lord Jesus Christ face eternal consequences.

Jesus' parable of the Wedding Banquet (Matthew 22:1–14) also illustrates the seriousness of rejecting God's invitation. Verse 7 states, "The king was furious, and he sent his army and destroyed those murderers and burned their city." The gospel is God's gracious invitation to all people to enter into a covenant relationship with Him through His Son, Jesus Christ. Those who reject that invitation will ultimately face His righteous judgment.

Consequence of Not Being Known by God

Just as it is important for us to know God, it is equally important that God knows us as His own. God will consummate His kingdom on earth when Jesus Christ returns in glory. Under the covenant, God’s ultimate promise and blessing is that His covenant people will inherit the kingdom of God. Jesus told the parable of the Ten Virgins (Matthew 25:1–13) to show that those who lack a genuine, intimate relationship with the Bridegroom will be denied entry into the kingdom of God.

The ten virgins represent the visible community of those who profess to be waiting for the Bridegroom’s return. Yet five are foolish, and five are wise. They are distinguished by their preparedness to receive the Bridegroom. All ten virgins had lamps, and all ten were waiting for the Bridegroom. The crucial difference was that the wise virgins were adequately prepared for his coming, whereas the foolish virgins were not.

When the foolish virgins finally arrived and pleaded, “Lord, lord, open to us,” the Bridegroom replied, “Truly, I say to you, I do not know you” (Matthew 25:11–12). .

Many interpret the exclusion of the five foolish virgins simply as the consequence of their lack of oil. However, the parable's deeper message is revealed in the Bridegroom’s words, “I do not know you.” Their exclusion was not merely about lacking oil; it ultimately exposed their lack of an intimate relationship with the Bridegroom. That, then, is the single reason they were unprepared to receive the Bridegroom.

Thus, the parable warns us that merely being among those who outwardly profess to await Christ’s return is not enough. What matters is being truly known by Christ—that is, having a genuine covenant relationship with Him. The decisive issue is not merely outward profession or association with the visible local church, but whether we truly belong to Christ.

During the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus warned His audience with the most terrifying words our Lord ever uttered. He said,

“Not everyone who says to me, ‘Lord, Lord,’ will enter the kingdom of heaven, but the one who does the will of my Father who is in heaven. On that day many will say to me, ‘Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and cast out demons in your name, and do many mighty works in your name?’ And then will I declare to them, ‘I never knew you; depart from me, you workers of lawlessness.’ (Matthew 7:21-23 ESV).

“On that day” refers to the Day of Judgment, as indicated by Matthew 25:31–46. Jesus was not directing these terrifying words merely to ordinary believers; rather, the warning is especially relevant to those who claim to serve Him as religious leaders, pastors, and ministers of His church.

In Scripture, repeating a person’s name twice often conveys emphasis, urgency, and a sense of personal address or intimacy. For example, in Genesis 22:11–12, God called Abraham by name twice: “Abraham! Abraham!” Likewise, in Exodus 3:4–5, God called Moses twice: “Moses, Moses!” In Luke 10:41–42, Jesus addressed Martha in the same way: “Martha, Martha.” Thus, repeating a name is not merely stylistic. It can emphasize the importance of the person being addressed and the seriousness or personal nature of the message.

What is the implication here? Jesus was saying that not everyone who addressed Him as “Lord, Lord” on the Day of Judgment would be saved. These people sincerely believed that they had an intimate personal relationship with the Lord. They were confident they would inherit the kingdom, but their hope proved in vain. In reality, the Lord Jesus had no personal relationship with them. He declares to them, “You who practice lawlessness.” The phrase “practice lawlessness” indicates that their lives were marked by violations of God's moral laws. Scripture defines sin as lawlessness (1 John 3:4). Despite their profession of faith and their religious activities, they had never truly known Christ.

Incidentally, they can be compared to the multitude of Israelites who perished in the wilderness during their sojourn. We read:

'Moreover, brethren, I do not want you to be unaware that all our fathers were under the cloud, all passed through the sea, all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, all ate the same spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink. For they drank of that spiritual Rock that followed them, and that Rock was Christ. But with most of them God was not well pleased, for their bodies were scattered in the wilderness.' (I Corinthians 10:1-5 NKJV).

The congregation in the wilderness included both true and false believers. The false believers participated outwardly in the same spiritual experiences as the true believers. They:

  1. Were under the cloud — meaning they were under the immediate presence and guidance of Yahweh.
  2. Passed through the sea — meaning they experienced deliverance from Pharaoh and his army.
  3. Were baptized into Moses — meaning they were identified with Moses and the covenant community through the Red Sea experience.
  4. Ate the same spiritual food — meaning they ate the manna God provided.
  5. Drank from the same spiritual Rock — meaning they received the water God miraculously provided from the rock, which Paul identifies as a spiritual source and associates with Christ.

Despite their spiritual experiences, God was displeased with the Israelites, and He put them to death in the wilderness.

Similarly, the visible local church may include both true and false believers. False believers may undergo spiritual experiences that appear similar to those of true believers. They are baptized; they fast and pray; they give tithes; they experience deliverance from evil spirits; they give testimonies; they participate in the Lord’s Supper; they speak in tongues; and so forth. Yet, despite all these outward experiences and religious activities, they may still lack an intimate personal relationship with the Lord.

As the parable of the ten virgins illustrates, it may be difficult to distinguish true believers from false believers. Outwardly, they may appear remarkably similar. Ultimately, however, God alone knows who truly belongs to Him and has an intimate relationship with Him. Therefore, the gospel should continue to be preached in the local church, because false believers may be among us and need an opportunity to repent, believe in Christ, and be saved.

The crucial question

Now a crucial question arises: Could it be possible for a true believer not to be known by God? It is impossible. As soon as a person becomes a true believer, he or she becomes an integral member of Christ’s risen body, the Church. And Christ, as the Head of that body, knows every one of its members intimately and completely.

Do You Really Know Who Jesus Is?

Jesus is the Son of God, the true promised Seed through whom God's promise to Father Abraham is fulfilled (Genesis 15:4–6; 18: 1-15; Galatians 4:21–31). The Holy Spirit conceived Him in the womb of the Virgin Mary. He was born in Bethlehem and raised in Nazareth. Because of this, people called Him "the Nazarene." In that region, people also called Him "the son of Mary," and some called Him "the carpenter's son." But who is Jesus in reality?

Jesus Himself answered this question in His testimony to the Jews:

"You are from beneath; I am from above. You are of this world; I am not of this world. Therefore, I said to you that you will die in your sins; for if you do not believe that I am He, you will die in your sins." (John 8:23–24, NKJV)

The Jews found it difficult to believe His testimony. Yet Jesus is exactly who He said He is. He is not of this world; He is from above—from heaven. God the Father sent Him to save sinners from their sins.

Jesus’ solemn words, "If you do not believe that I am He, you will die in your sins," make clear that salvation depends on believing in His true identity. The expression "I am He" points to Jesus as the One sent by the Father—the Sent One (cf. Siloam, meaning "Sent"). Therefore, for a sinner to be saved, he must know and believe that Jesus is the Son of God, sent by the Father as the promised Messiah, the Christ.

Scripture reveals three key witnesses to who Jesus really is. They are (1) God the Father, (2) the miracles (or works) that He performed, and (3) the Spirit of Prophecy, or the Sacred Scripture.

God the Father

At His baptism in the River Jordan at the hands of John the Baptist, as Jesus came out of the water, the heavens were opened to Him, and He saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and alighting upon Him. Suddenly, a voice came from heaven, saying, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.” (Matthew 3:15-16, NKJV). The other two gospel writers also recorded this incident in their respective gospel accounts (Mark 1:10-11; Luke 3:21-22).

On another occasion, Jesus took three of His disciples, Peter, James, and John, to a mountaintop and transfigured before them, revealing His true being. The disciples describe His transfiguration this way: “His face shone like the sun, and His clothes became as white as the light.” Then, suddenly, a voice came from the cloud, saying, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Hear Him!” (Matthew 17:5, NKJV). This incident is also recorded in Mark (9:2-8) and Luke (9:28-36). And the eyewitness Peter also recorded this incident in his second epistle (2 Peter 1:16-18).

Thus, on two occasions, God the Father declared in an audible voice that Jesus Christ, who appeared in human form, is His most dearly beloved Son. Thus, we come to know that the being of Jesus Christ is the eternal God, the Son.

Incidentally, when Scripture says “the Son of God,” it does not mean that Jesus is God's biological son. On the contrary, it means that Jesus reflects God the Father in every respect here on earth. It means that Jesus is of the same substance (or essence) as the Father. Scripture testifies to this truth in this manner:

'He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation. For by Him all things were created that are in heaven and that are on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones or dominions or principalities or powers. All things were created through Him and for Him. And He is before all things, and in Him all things consist.' (Colossians 1:15-17, NKJV).

'For in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily' (Colossians 2:9, NKJV).

Apostle John testifies, 'And the Word (a reference to God, the Son) became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth' (John 1:14, NKJV).

If we liken God the Father to a mold, then the casting that comes out of the mold is the Son of God. [Please note that this comparison is intended to illustrate a specific aspect, namely reflecting the same essence as the Father, rather than every aspect of the relationship.]

We should recognize that Jesus Christ's divine sonship did not begin with His incarnation. He was the Son of God before His incarnation, during His incarnation, and after His resurrection. Jesus Christ is the eternal Son of God. He is one in being with the Father and the Holy Spirit, from eternity to eternity.

The resurrected Christ revealed Himself to His dear disciple John and testified about Himself, saying:

“I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End,” says the Lord, “who is and who was and who is to come, the Almighty" (Revelation 1:8, NKJV).

“I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End, the First and the Last” (Revelation 22:13, NKJV).

Just as every point on a circle can be regarded as both its beginning and its end, so it is with Jesus Christ, the eternal Son of God. He is “the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End, the First and the Last,” meaning that He is eternal—everlasting.

The Miracles Jesus Performed

We should also recognize that the miracles Jesus performed are miracles only God, the Creator, can perform. And those miracles were pre-ordained and recorded in the prophetic books of Scripture (cf. Isaiah 41 & 61). Jesus never performed a miracle to gain popularity. He did not perform a single miracle of His own. All the miracles He performed were given to Him by His Father. The purpose of the miracles was to authenticate the Messenger. Yet most miracles also met people's dire needs in line with the circumstances.

How do we know the miracles testify to Jesus' person? Jesus Himself said,

“But I have a greater witness than John’s; for the works which the Father has given Me to finish—the very works that I do—bear witness of Me, that the Father has sent Me. And the Father Himself, who sent Me, has testified of Me. You have neither heard His voice at any time, nor seen His form” (John 5:36-37, NKJV).

Jesus, in fact, performed many more miracles than those recorded in the gospels (John21: 25). Apostle John says,

“And truly Jesus did many other signs in the presence of His disciples, which are not written in this book; but these are written that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in His name” (John 20:30-31, NKJV).

He makes clear that the miracles (or signs) Jesus performed attest to His identity as the Christ (or the Messiah) sent by God the Father.

Of all the miracles Jesus performed during His earthly ministry, the following are outstanding and attest to His identity:

  1. The miracle of raising Lazarus after four days of his death and burial, recorded in John 11.
  2. The miracle of opening the eyes of a man who was born blind, recorded in John 9.
  3. The miracle of cleansing a leper, recorded in Mark 1: 40-45.
  4. The miracle of feeding the five thousand men, recorded in Mark 1: 30-44.
  5. The miracle of calming the raging sea and wind, recorded in Mark 4:35-41.
  6. The miracle of turning plain water into a superior wine, recorded in John 2:1-11.
  7. The miracle of walking on water, recorded in Matthew 14: 22-23.
  8. The numerous cases of casting out demons and evil spirits from people who were oppressed by these dark, evil forces (Matthew 8:31-32; 9:33; Mark 5:8; 16:9; Luke 4:41; 11:14; etc.).

The Spirit of Prophecy (or the Sacred Scripture)

Jesus did not appear in history unexpectedly. His coming into the world fulfilled God's redemptive plan, revealed through Old Testament prophecies recorded centuries before His birth. Scripture foretold His virgin birth, earthly ministry, suffering, crucifixion, and resurrection, and fulfilled them in remarkable detail.

In the eternal counsel of the triune God concerning the plan of redemption, the Son of God said to the Father:

"Sacrifice and offering You did not desire; My ears You have opened" (that is, as a bondservant). "Burnt offering and sin offering You did not require. Then I said, 'Behold, I come; in the scroll of the book it is written of Me. I delight to do Your will, O My God, and Your law is within My heart'" (Psalm 40:6–8, NKJV).

These words reveal the Son's willing submission to the Father's redemptive purpose, foreshadowing His incarnation and obedient sacrifice for humanity's salvation.

The writer of Hebrews interprets the above-quoted scripture this way: 'Therefore, when He came into the world, He said: “Sacrifice and offering You did not desire, but a body You have prepared for Me. In burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin, You had no pleasure. Then I said, ‘Behold, I have come— In the volume of the book it is written of Me— To do Your will, O God.’ ” Previously saying, “Sacrifice and offering, burnt offerings, and offerings for sin You did not desire, nor had pleasure in them” (which are offered according to the law), then He said, “Behold, I have come to do Your will, O God.” He takes away the first that He may establish the second. By that will we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.' (Hebrews 10:5-10, NKJV).

No other founder of a major religion, religious leader, prophet, sage, or spiritual teacher is presented as fulfilling a long-established body of prophecies concerning his birth, life, ministry, death, and resurrection. Jesus alone came as the promised Messiah, fulfilling what God had declared beforehand through His prophets.

These fulfilled prophecies provide compelling testimony to Jesus' unique identity. They bear witness that He is not merely a great teacher or prophet, but God the Son, the promised Messiah. As Scripture declares, "For the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy" (Revelation 19:10b, NKJV). From the beginning, biblical prophecy points to Christ, and in Him those promises find their fulfillment.

What happened at Jesus Christ’s conception?

It is established beyond doubt that the being of Jesus Christ is God the Son. But now a question arises: ‘How did the Son of God become man?’

When Gabriel, God's messenger, announced to the Virgin Mary that she would conceive and bear a son, she was confused. So she asked the angel, “How will this be, since I am a virgin?” (Luke 1:34).

And the angel answered and said to her, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Highest will overshadow you; therefore, also, that Holy One who is to be born will be called the Son of God" (Luke 1:35, NKJV).

So, the first inference to draw is that the Holy Spirit (i.e., the Power of God) made it possible for the Son of God to be conceived in the womb of the Virgin Mary. At His conception, the Son of God became truly human. What do you mean by ‘truly human’? He took upon Himself a true human body with a human soul. If Jesus did not have a human soul, He would not have cried out, saying, “My soul is exceedingly sorrowful, even to death. Stay here and watch with Me.” (Matthew 26:38, NKJV). Obviously, a human soul is essential for the Son of God to experience human emotions and pain.

For our salvation, the Savior had to assume human nature in its entirety. Human nature includes a body, a soul—with its will, emotions, and intellect (mind)—and a spirit. To redeem humanity, a sinless, complete human being had to offer Himself to God in perfect obedience as a sacrificial substitute for sinners.

By assuming our full humanity, Christ entered the realm of death on our behalf to redeem those under its power. His true human nature qualified Him to act as our substitute and to deliver us from the bondage of eternal death.

The writer of Hebrews affirms this truth:

"Inasmuch then as the children have partaken of flesh and blood, He Himself likewise shared in the same, that through death He might destroy him who had the power of death, that is, the devil... For indeed He does not give aid to angels, but He does give aid to the seed of Abraham." (Hebrews 2:14, 16, NKJV)

The sinless nature of Jesus

We have seen that, by assuming true human nature yet remaining without sin, Jesus was qualified to become the Messiah—the Rescuer of sinners. Sacred Scripture affirms that Jesus was sinless:

Jesus, the High Priest, is without sin: 'Seeing then that we have a great High Priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession. For we do not have a High Priest who cannot sympathize with our weaknesses, but was in all points tempted as we are, yet without sin. ' (Hebrews 4:14-15, NKJV)

Jesus committed no sin: 'For to this you were called, because Christ also suffered for us, leaving us an example, that you should follow His steps: “Who committed no sin, Nor was deceit found in His mouth”; who, when He was reviled, did not revile in return; when He suffered, He did not threaten, but committed Himself to Him who judges righteously; ' (I Peter 2:21-23, NKJV).

Jesus knew no sin: 'For He (i.e., God the Father) made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God in Him.' (2 Corinthians 5:21, NKJV).

In Jesus there is no sin: 'And you know that He was manifested to take away our sins, and in Him there is no sin. ' (I John 3:5, NKJV).

We should recognize that although Jesus was sinless in His human nature, He could have sinned. Therefore, Satan continued to tempt Jesus throughout His earthly ministry, culminating in the events leading to His death on the cross. Yet Jesus overcame every temptation and remained sinless.

But who made Jesus sinless?

Some Christian denominations teach that Mary was preserved from original sin and, therefore, was without sin. They argue that this was necessary for Jesus to be born without sin. However, this doctrine is inconsistent with the gospel of Jesus Christ and undermines its central message. Mary was a member of the Adamic race who, by God's grace and favor, was chosen to bear the Savior in her womb. Like every other descendant of Adam, she was born with the same Adamic (sinful) nature inherited from her parents and forefathers.

But who caused Jesus to be born sinless? In the womb of the Virgin Mary, God the Father caused the Son of God to assume a sinless human nature. We should know that the first man, Adam, was sinless before his fall. In the Incarnation, the Son of God took on the same sinless human nature while retaining His divine nature.

The being of Jesus, the Christ, defined

Every professing Christian must know and acknowledge who the historical Jesus is. As theologian R.C. Sproul says, "The truth about Jesus, not mere opinion, matters . . . and it matters eternally."

Christian faith is built on confessional theology (Matthew 16:13-19). I strongly recommend that believers study and affirm the truth about Jesus Christ as set forth in the following ‘Christology Statement’.

Christology Statement [Source: Ligioneer Ministries]

We confess the mystery and wonder of God made flesh and rejoice in our great salvation through Jesus Christ our Lord. With the Father and the Holy Spirit, the Son created all things, sustains all things, and makes all things new. Truly God, He became truly man, two natures in one person.

He was born of the Virgin Mary and lived among us. Crucified, dead, and buried, He rose on the third day, ascended to heaven, and will come again in glory and judgment.

For us, He kept the Law, atoned for sin, and satisfied God’s wrath. He took our filthy rags and gave us His righteous robe. He is our Prophet, Priest, and King, building His church, interceding for us, and reigning over all things.

Jesus Christ is Lord; we praise His holy Name forever. Amen.

The Chalcedonian Definition

‘The Chalcedonian Definition is the declaration of Christ as fully God and fully human, having two natures "without confusion, without change, without division, without separation", adopted at the Council of Chalcedon in AD 451. Chalcedon was an early center of Christianity located in Asia Minor.’ [Source: Wikipedia]. Therefore, it is worthwhile to study and affirm the Chalcedonian Definition given below:

The Chalcedonian Definition
AD 451

“Therefore, following the holy fathers, we all with one accord teach men to acknowledge one and the same Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, at once complete in Godhead and complete in manhood, truly God and truly man, consisting also of a reasonable soul and body; of one substance with the Father as regards his Godhead, and at the same time of one substance with us as regards his manhood; like us in all respects, apart from sin; as regards his Godhead, begotten of the Father before the ages, but yet as regards his manhood begotten, for us men and for our salvation, of Mary the Virgin, the God-bearer; one and the same Christ, Son, Lord, Only-begotten, recognized in two natures, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation; the distinction of natures being in no way annulled by the union, but rather the characteristics of each nature being preserved and coming together to form one person and subsistence, not as parted or separated into two persons, but one and the same Son and Only-begotten God the Word, Lord Jesus Christ; even as the prophets from earliest times spoke of him, and our Lord Jesus Christ himself taught us, and the creed of the fathers has handed down to us.”
