The Necessity of Knowing God

The Necessity of Knowing God

Alex M. Kuruvilla

God desires His people to know Him intimately and personally because He is a personal and relational God. Rather than remaining distant from humanity, He enters into a covenant relationship with His people. Those who are in this covenant relationship are recipients of His steadfast love (His hesed), through which He faithfully pours out His blessings upon His covenant partners.

Scripture portrays this covenant relationship as being like that of a husband and wife. Just as a husband comes to know his wife through a deep, personal, and intimate relationship, God knows His people in the same way. In return, He calls His people to know Him with that same depth of intimacy and devotion. The crux of the Christian faith is knowing God and being known by Him.

For this reason, God invites every believer to pursue a personal and intimate relationship with Him, to grow in the knowledge of who He is, and to experience the fullness of His covenant love.

The following Bible verses attest to this truth:

‘Let us know; let us press on to know the Lord; his going out is sure as the dawn; he will come to us as the showers, as the spring rains that water the earth.’ (Hosea 6:3)

‘For I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice, the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings.’ (Hosea 6:6)

‘My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge (i.e., knowledge of God); because you have rejected knowledge (i.e., knowledge of God), I reject you from being a priest to me. And since you have forgotten the law of your God, I also will forget your children.’ (Hosea 4:6)

‘The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, and the knowledge of the Holy One is understanding (or wisdom).’ (Proverbs 9:10)

‘Thus says the Lord: “Let not the wise man boast in his wisdom, let not the mighty man boast in his might, let not the rich man boast in his riches, but let him who boasts boast in this, that he understands and knows me, that I am the Lord who practices steadfast love, justice, and righteousness in the earth. For in these things I delight, declares the Lord.”’ (Jeremiah 9:23-24)

‘Oh, taste and see that the Lord is good! Blessed is the man who takes refuge in him! (Psalm 34:8).

’ For thus says the Lord to the house of Israel: “Seek me and live;” (Amos 5:4).

Seeking involves knowing God intimately and personally.

On the night when our Lord was betrayed, He prayed, ‘And this is eternal life, that they know You, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom You have sent’ (John 17:3).

The Surpassing Worth of Knowing Christ Jesus

Apostle Paul (Jewish name: Saul) was a Pharisee and the son of a Pharisee. He was zealous about his ancestral rich traditions. Historians testify that he was a rich man, too. But he counted all these losses for the sake of knowing Jesus. Such is the surpassing worthiness of knowing Jesus Christ, our Lord.

Apostle Paul, writing to the Philippian church, said,

“But whatever gain I had, I counted as loss for the sake of Christ. Indeed, I count everything as loss because of the surpassing worth of knowing Christ Jesus, my Lord. For his sake I have suffered the loss of all things and count them as rubbish, in order that I may gain Christ and be found in him, not having a righteousness of my own that comes from the law, but that which comes through faith in Christ, the righteousness from God that depends on faith— that I may know him and the power of his resurrection, and may share his sufferings, becoming like him in his death, that by any means possible I may attain the resurrection from the dead.” (Philippians 3:7-11).

Knowing Christ, the Basis of Christian Faith

The Christian faith is not founded on blind belief but on the sure knowledge of the historical Jesus, whom God sent in fulfillment of the Scriptures, and on the confession of Him as Lord (Matthew 16:15–16; Romans 10:9–10). Jesus Himself declared that eternal life consists in knowing the only true God and Jesus Christ, whom He sent to save sinners from their sins (John 17:3). Therefore, knowing Jesus Christ is essential to trusting Him and believing in His saving work.

This emphasis on knowing God is consistent throughout Scripture. As the Lord declares through the prophet Hosea, “For I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice, the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings” (Hosea 6:6). Although the LORD God Yahweh Himself ordained the burnt offering and described it as “a pleasing aroma” to Him (Leviticus 1: 9, 17), He values the knowledge of God even more. This demonstrates that while outward acts of worship have their proper place, they are never a substitute for a genuine, personal knowledge of God.

Who is Jesus?

Jesus is the Son of God, the true promised Seed through whom God’s promise to Father Abraham is fulfilled (Genesis 15:4–6; 18: 1-15; Galatians 4:21–31). He was born of the Virgin Mary by the power of the Holy Spirit. He was born in Bethlehem and raised in Nazareth. Because of this, He was called “the Nazarene.” In that region, He was also known as “the son of Mary,” and some referred to Him as “the carpenter’s son.” But who is Jesus in reality?

Jesus Himself answered this question in His testimony to the Jews:

“You are from beneath; I am from above. You are of this world; I am not of this world. Therefore, I said to you that you will die in your sins; for if you do not believe that I am He, you will die in your sins.” (John 8:23–24, NKJV)

The Jews found it difficult to believe His testimony. Yet Jesus is exactly who He declared Himself to be. He is not of this world; He is from above — from heaven. He was sent by God the Father to save sinners from their sins.

His solemn words, “If you do not believe that I am He, you will die in your sins,” make clear that salvation depends on believing in His true identity. The expression “I am He” points to Jesus as the One sent by the Father — the Sent One (cf. Siloam, meaning “Sent”). Therefore, for a sinner to be saved, he must know and believe that Jesus is the Son of God, sent by the Father as the promised Messiah, the Christ.

Scripture reveals that the most important witnesses to who Jesus really is are three. They are

  1. God the Father
  2. the works that He performed
  3. the Spirit of Prophecy, or the Sacred Scripture

1. God the Father

At His baptism in the River Jordan at the hands of John the Baptist, as Jesus came out of the water, the heavens were opened to Him, and He saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and alighting upon Him. And suddenly a voice came from heaven, saying, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.” (Matthew 3: 15-16, NKJV). The other two gospel writers also recorded this incident in their respective gospel accounts (Mark 1: 10-11; Luke 3: 21-22).

On another occasion, Jesus took three of His disciples, Peter, James, and John, to a mountaintop and transfigured before them, revealing His true being. The disciples describe His transfiguration in these words: “His face shone like the sun, and His clothes became as white as the light.” Then, suddenly, a voice came from the cloud, saying, “This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. Hear Him!” (Matthew 17:5, NKJV). This incident is also recorded in the gospels of Mark (9:2-8) and Luke (9:28-36). And the eyewitness Peter also recorded this incident in his second epistle (2 Peter 1:16-18).

Thus, on two occasions, God the Father declared in an audible voice that Jesus Christ, who appeared in human form, is His most dearly beloved Son. Thus, we come to know that the being of Jesus Christ is the eternal God, the Son.

Incidentally, when the Scripture says “the Son of God”, it does not mean that Jesus is the biological son of God. On the contrary, it means that Jesus reflects God the Father in every respect here on earth. It means that Jesus is of the same substance (or essence) as the Father. Scripture testifies to this truth in this manner:

“He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation. For by Him all things were created that are in heaven and that are on earth, visible and invisible, whether thrones ordominions or principalities or powers. All things were created through Him and for Him. And He is before all things, and in Him all things consist.” (Colossians 1:15-17, NKJV).

“For in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily” (Colossians 2:9, NKJV).

Apostle John testifies, ‘And the Word (a reference to God, the Son) became flesh and dwelt among us, and we beheld His glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth’ (John 1:14, NKJV).

If we liken God the Father to a mold, then the casting that comes out of the mold is the Son of God. [Please note that this comparison is intended to illustrate a specific aspect, namely reflecting the same essence as the Father, rather than every aspect of the relationship.]

We should recognize that the divine sonship of Jesus Christ did not originate with His incarnation. He was the Son of God before His incarnation, He was the Son of God during His incarnation, and He remains the Son of God after His resurrection. Jesus Christ is the eternal Son of God. He is one in being with the Father and the Holy Spirit, from eternity to eternity.

The resurrected Christ revealed Himself to His dear disciple John and testified about Himself, saying:

“I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End,” says the Lord, “who is and who was and who is to come, the Almighty” (Revelation 1:8, NKJV).

“I am the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End, the First and the Last” (Revelation 22:13, NKJV).

Just as every point on a circle can be regarded as both its beginning and its end, so it is with Jesus Christ, the eternal Son of God. He is “the Alpha and the Omega, the Beginning and the End, the First and the Last,” meaning that He is eternal — everlasting.

2. The Miracles Jesus Performed

We should also recognize that the miracles Jesus performed are miracles only God, the Creator, can perform. And those miracles were pre-ordained and recorded in the prophetic books of the scripture (cf. Isaiah 41 & 61). Jesus never performed a miracle to gain popularity. He did not perform a single miracle of His own that was not given to Him by His Father. The objective behind the miracles was to convey to the people that He is the promised Messiah sent by God the Father. Yet most miracles also met the people’s dire needs in accordance with prevailing circumstances.

How do we know the miracles testify to the person of Jesus? Jesus Himself said,

‘But I have a greater witness than John’s; for the works which the Father has given Me to finish — the very works that I do — bear witness of Me, that the Father has sent Me. And the Father Himself, who sent Me, has testified of Me. You have neither heard His voice at any time, nor seen His form’ (John 5:36-37, NKJV).

Jesus, in fact, performed many more miracles than those recorded in the gospels (John 21: 25). Apostle John says, ‘And truly Jesus did many other signs in the presence of His disciples, which are not written in this book; but these are written that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in His name’ (John 20:30-31, NKJV). He makes it clear that the miracles (or signs) performed by Jesus attest to His identity as the Christ (or the Messiah) sent by God the Father.

Of all the miracles Jesus performed during His earthly ministry, the following are outstanding and attesting to His identity:

  1. The miracle of raising Lazarus after four days of his death and burial, recorded in John 11.
  2. The miracle of opening the eyes of a man who was born blind, recorded in John 9.
  3. The miracle of cleansing a leper, recorded in Mark 1: 40-45.
  4. The miracle of feeding the five thousand men, recorded in Mark 1: 30-44.
  5. The miracle of calming the raging sea and the wind, recorded in Mark 4: 35-41.
  6. The miracle of infusing plain water with the properties of a superior wine, recorded in John 2: 1-1.
  7. The miracle of walking on water, recorded in Matthew 14: 22-23.
  8. The numerous cases of casting out demons and evil spirits from people who were oppressed by these dark, evil forces (Matthew 8:31-32; 9:33; Mark 5:8; 16:9; Luke 4:41; 11:14; etc.).

3. The Spirit of Prophecy (or the Sacred Scripture)

Jesus did not appear in history unexpectedly. His coming into the world was the fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan, revealed through Old Testament prophecies recorded centuries before His birth. His virgin birth, earthly ministry, suffering, crucifixion, and resurrection were all foretold in Scripture and fulfilled in remarkable detail.

In the eternal counsel of the triune God concerning the plan of redemption, the Son of God said to the Father:

“Sacrifice and offering You did not desire; My ears You have opened” (that is, as a bondservant). “Burnt offering and sin offering You did not require. Then I said, ‘Behold, I come; in the scroll of the book it is written of Me. I delight to do Your will, O My God, and Your law is within My heart’” (Psalm 40:6–8, NKJV).

These words reveal the Son’s willing submission to the Father’s redemptive purpose, foreshadowing His incarnation and obedient sacrifice for the salvation of humanity.

It is interesting to note that the above-quoted scripture is interpreted by the writer of the Hebrews in this manner:

“Therefore, when He came into the world, He said: ‘Sacrifice and offering You did not desire, But a body You have prepared for Me. In burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin, You had no pleasure. Then I said, “Behold, I have come — In the volume of the book it is written of Me — To do Your will, O God.”’ Previously saying, ‘Sacrifice and offering, burnt offerings, and offerings for sin You did not desire, nor had pleasure in them’ (which are offered according to the law), then He said, ‘Behold, I have come to do Your will, O God.’ He takes away the first that He may establish the second. By that will we have been sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all.” (Hebrews 10:5-10, NKJV).

No other founder of a major religion, religious leader, prophet, sage, or spiritual teacher is presented as fulfilling a long-established body of prophecies concerning his birth, life, ministry, death, and resurrection. Jesus alone came as the promised Messiah, fulfilling what God had declared beforehand through His prophets.

These fulfilled prophecies provide compelling testimony to Jesus’ unique identity. They bear witness that He is not merely a great teacher or prophet, but God the Son, the promised Messiah. As the Scriptures declare, “For the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy” (Revelation 19:10b, NKJV). From the beginning, biblical prophecy points to Christ, and in Him those promises find their fulfillment.

What happened at Jesus Christ’s conception?

It has been established, beyond any doubt, that the being of Jesus Christ is God the Son. But now a question arises: ‘How did the Son of God become man?’

When Gabriel, the messenger from God, announced to the Virgin Mary that she would conceive and bear a son, she was confused. So she asked the angel, “How will this be, since I am a virgin?” (Luke 1:34). And the angel answered and said to her, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Highest will overshadow you; therefore, also, that Holy One who is to be born will be called the Son of God” (Luke 1:35, NKJV).

So, the first inference to draw is that the Holy Spirit (the Power of God) made it possible for the Son of God to be conceived in the womb of the Virgin Mary. At His conception, the Son of God became truly human. What do you mean by ‘truly human’? He took upon Himself a true human body with a human soul. If Jesus did not have a human soul, He would not have cried out, saying, “My soul is exceedingly sorrowful, even to death. Stay here and watch with Me.” (Matthew 26:38, NKJV). Obviously, a human soul is essential for the Son of God to experience human emotions and pain.

For our salvation, it was necessary for the Savior to assume human nature in its entirety. Human nature includes a body, a soul — with its will, emotions, and intellect (mind) — and a spirit. To redeem humanity, a sinless and complete human being had to offer Himself to God in perfect obedience as the sacrificial substitute for sinners.

By assuming our full humanity, Christ entered the realm of death on our behalf in order to redeem those who were under its power. His true human nature qualified Him to act as our representative and to deliver us from the bondage of eternal death.

The writer of Hebrews affirms this truth:

“Inasmuch then as the children have partaken of flesh and blood, He Himself likewise shared in the same, that through death He might destroy him who had the power of death, that is, the devil… For indeed He does not give aid to angels, but He does give aid to the seed of Abraham.” (Hebrews 2:14, 16, NKJV)

How Did Jesus Acquire The Sinless Nature?

We have seen that it is the sinless true human nature of Jesus that qualified Him to become the Messiah, the rescuer of sinners. Sacred Scripture affirms that Jesus was sinless:

Jesus, the High Priest, is without sin: “Seeing then that we have a great High Priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our confession. For we do not have a High Priest who cannot sympathize with our weaknesses, but was in all points tempted as we are, yet without sin.” (Hebrews 4:14-15, NKJV)

Jesus committed no sin: “For to this you were called, because Christ also suffered for us, leaving us an example, that you should follow His steps: ‘Who committed no sin, Nor was deceit found in His mouth’; who, when He was reviled, did not revile in return; when He suffered, He did not threaten, but committed Himself to Him who judges righteously;” (I Peter 2:21-23, NKJV).

Jesus knew no sin: “For He (i.e., God the Father) made Him who knew no sin to be sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God in Him.” (2 Corinthians 5:21, NKJV).

In Jesus there is no sin: 'And you know that He was manifested to take away our sins, and in Him there is no sin.' (I John 3:5, NKJV).

We should recognize that although Jesus, in His human nature, was sinless, He was capable of sinning. Therefore, Satan continued to tempt Jesus throughout His earthly ministry, culminating in the events leading to His death on the cross. Yet Jesus overcame every temptation and remained sinless.

But Who Made Jesus Sinless?

Some people attribute Jesus’ sinless nature to the sinlessness of Mary, His mother. However, this doctrine is inconsistent with the gospel of Jesus Christ and undermines its central message. Mary was a member of the Adamic race who, by God’s grace and favor, was chosen to bear the Savior in her womb. Like every other descendant of Adam, she was born with the same Adamic (sinful) nature inherited from her parents and forefathers.

In the womb of the Virgin Mary, God the Father caused the Son of God to assume a sinless human nature. We should know that the first man, Adam, was created sinless before he disobeyed God. In the Incarnation, the Son of God took on that same kind of sinless human nature while retaining His divine nature.

The Being Of Jesus, The Christ, Defined

It is imperative that every professing Christian know and acknowledge who the historical Jesus is. As R.C. Sproul says, “The truth about Jesus, not mere opinion, matters… and it matters eternally.”

Christian faith is built on confessional theology (Matthew 16:13-19). I strongly recommend that believers study and affirm the truth about Jesus Christ as set forth in the following ‘Christology Statement’ [Source: Ligioneer Ministries].

Christology Statement

We confess the mystery and wonder of God made flesh and rejoice in our great salvation through Jesus Christ our Lord. With the Father and the Holy Spirit, the Son created all things, sustains all things, and makes all things new. Truly God, He became truly man, two natures in one person.

He was born of the Virgin Mary and lived among us. Crucified, dead, and buried, He rose on the third day, ascended to heaven, and will come again in glory and judgment.

For us, He kept the Law, atoned for sin, and satisfied God’s wrath. He took our filthy rags and gave us His righteous robe. He is our Prophet, Priest, and King, building His church, interceding for us, and reigning over all things.

Jesus Christ is Lord; we praise His holy Name forever. Amen.

The Chalcedonian Definition

‘The Chalcedonian Definition is the declaration of Christ as fully God and fully human, having two natures “without confusion, without change, without division, without separation”, adopted at the Council of Chalcedon in AD 451. Chalcedon was an early center of Christianity located in Asia Minor.’ [Source: Wikipedia].

Therefore, it is worthwhile to study the Chalcedonian Definition given below:

The Chalcedonian Definition

“Therefore, following the holy fathers, we all with one accord teach men to acknowledge one and the same Son, our Lord Jesus Christ, at once complete in Godhead and complete in manhood, truly God and truly man, consisting also of a reasonable soul and body; of one substance with the Father as regards his Godhead, and at the same time of one substance with us as regards his manhood; like us in all respects, apart from sin; as regards his Godhead, begotten of the Father before the ages, but yet as regards his manhood begotten, for us men and for our salvation, of Mary the Virgin, the God-bearer; one and the same Christ, Son, Lord, Only-begotten, recognized in two natures, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation; the distinction of natures being in no way annulled by the union, but rather the characteristics of each nature being preserved and coming together to form one person and subsistence, not as parted or separated into two persons, but one and the same Son and Only-begotten God the Word, Lord Jesus Christ; even as the prophets from earliest times spoke of him, and our Lord Jesus Christ himself taught us, and the creed of the fathers has handed down to us.”
