﻿﻿The Conflicting Natures Of A Saved Sinner

An Exposition of Romans 7:12-25

Alex M. Kuruvilla

Scripture:

12“Therefore, the law is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good. 13Has then what is good become death to me? Certainly not! But sin, that it might appear sin, was producing death in me through what is good, so that sin through the commandment might become exceedingly sinful. 14For we know that the law is spiritual, but I am carnal, sold under sin. 15For what I am doing, I do not understand. For what I will to do, that I do not practice; but what I hate, that I do.

16If, then, I do what I will not to do, I agree with the law that it is good. 17But now, it is no longer I who do it, but sin that dwells in me. 18For I know that in me (that is, in my flesh) nothing good dwells; for to will is present with me, but how to perform what is good I do not find. 19For the good that I will to do, I do not do; but the evil I will not to do, that I practice. 20Now if I do what I will not to do, it is no longer I who do it, but sin that dwells in me. 21I find then a law, that evil is present with me, the one who wills to do good. 22For I delight in the law of God according to the inward man. 23But I see another law in my members, warring against the law of my mind, and bringing me into captivity to the law of sin which is in my members.

24O wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from this body of death? 25I thank God—through Jesus Christ our Lord! So then, with the mind I myself serve the law of God, but with the flesh the law of sin.“
(Romans 7:12-25 NKJV).

Explanation:

Vv 12-13: Confirms that the law (ന്യായപ്രമാണം) is holy, and the commandment (i.e., the first commandment to Adam and the Ten Commandments) is holy, just, and good. How? Because they proceeded from the mouth of Holy, Just, and Good God. The commandments reveal God Himself, who is holy, just, and good (Illustrate). Then, how come that which is good caused death in me? It is not the commandment that caused death; it is sin (i.e., the personification of sin - Satan) who caused death in man by enticing him to break the commandment. Jesus said that the Devil (Satan) is a murderer from the beginning (John 8:44). Then, why did God give the commandment? Because He wants to demonstrate how utterly sinful (evilish) Satan is (illustrate).

Vv 14-21: In the context of Satan using the commandment as an instrument to bring about death in Adam and in the Adamic race, Paul is defending the law, theTen Commandments. He says that the law is spiritual. How? Because it is holy, just, and good, and it reveals the holy God. He says, “Therefore, I am for it, meaning I want to do what the law commands. But I can’t do it”. Why? “Because I am carnal, and sold under sin”. ‘Sold under sin’ means sold under the slavery of sin (i.e., Satan). What is the peculiarity of being a slave of Mr. Sin (Satan)? My will to obey God (i.e., to do good) cannot be fulfilled because of the slavary, and it is my Master’s will that I am compelled to fulfill (illustrate). It is against my will that I disobey God (or do not do the good). As a slave sold under sin, I am compelled to act against my will because of the sinful nature within me. I have come to realize that nothing good dwells in me; rather, it is sin—the evil—that lives within me (Romans 3: 11-12).

Vv 22-23: Here, in these verses, Paul says that with his mind (i.e., the inward man), he rejoices in the law of God. But at the same time, there is another law that is warring against the law of his mind. As a saved sinner, he is caught between two opposing forces: one, the willingness to do good (i.e., the willingness to obey God’s commandments), and the other, doing evil (i.e., breaking God’s commandments), contrary to his will. That, in fact, is the status quo of a saved sinner. The status quo of a saved sinner is that he is righteous and sinful simultaneously.

V24: ‘O wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from this body of death?’ Being pulled by the forces of these laws in opposite directions, Paul considers himself a miserable, distressed man. His experience is like that of a living man tightly tied to a rotten, dead body. The term, ‘this body of death,’ means ‘this body of sinful nature’, ‘this mortal body’, ‘the flesh’. The deliverance that he expects is not temporary deliverance, but permanent. The final deliverance must be from the fleshly constraints of this life. Here, the term ‘death’ refers to the physical death and death caused by the craftiness of Sin (Satan). We read in 1 Corinthians 15: 26, 'The last enemy that will be destroyed is death. ' It is the final outworking and end of death’s rule over this age, and so the defeat of the last enemy for which Paul longs here.

V25: ‘I thank God—through Jesus Christ our Lord! So then, with the mind I myself serve the law of God, but with the flesh the law of sin.' Paul is thanking God for Jesus Christ's role as God’s agent in bringing about the final deliverance. Apart from Christ, the law cannot liberate us from the dominance of sin and death.

About the final deliverance, we read, 'Behold, I tell you a mystery: We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed— in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed. For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality. So when this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality, then shall be brought to pass the saying that is written: “Death is swallowed up in victory.” “O Death, where is your sting? O Hades, where is your victory?” The sting of death is sin, and the strength of sin is the law. But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.' (1 Corinthians 15:51-57 NKJV).

In the beginning, although Satan used God’s commandment to Adam and Eve as a tool to bring about sin and death to humanity, God employed a better strategy to destroy sin and death once and for all. In the death of Jesus Christ on the cross, He killed ‘death’, the consequence of sin, once and for all. Hence, we read: 'And I heard a loud voice from heaven saying, “Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and He will dwell with them, and they shall be His people. God Himself will be with them and be their God. And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes; there shall be no more death, nor sorrow, nor crying. There shall be no more pain, for the former things have passed away.” Then He who sat on the throne said, “Behold, I make all things new.” And He said to me, “Write, for these words are true and faithful.” (Revelation 21:3-5 NKJV ).

Glory to God. Oh what a blessed salvation!!
